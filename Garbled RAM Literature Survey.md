# Garbled RAM: a literature survey

Garbled RAM is the RAM-model analogue of Yao's garbled circuits: a garbler emits, in one message, an object whose size is proportional to a program's running time rather than to the size of a circuit that could compute it, and an evaluator runs it without further interaction. The idea was introduced by [Lu and Ostrovsky 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-38348-9_42), and thirteen years later the state of the art is a family of constructions whose bandwidth per memory access has fallen from a PRF circuit times several logarithms to, for large words, the bandwidth of an optimal oblivious RAM with no security-parameter factor at all ([Gu, Ghoshal and Shi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-25324-8_10); [Fickes, He and Lin 2026](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1998)). The field went through three phases: a foundational phase from 2013 to 2018 that repaired the original proof and pinned down assumptions, adaptivity, parallelism and succinctness; a practicality phase in 2022 and 2023 that removed cryptography from inside the garbled circuit and brought overhead to a few logarithms; and a current phase, from 2024 on, that trades assumptions against the last logarithmic and security-parameter factors and extends the primitive to malicious, multiparty and adaptive settings at essentially semi-honest cost.

## Origins and foundations

### From oblivious RAM to a garbled RAM definition

Secure computation of RAM programs predates garbling by fifteen years and was interactive throughout. The problem is that Yao's protocol ([Yao 1986](https://doi.org/10.1109/sfcs.1986.25)), formalized as a garbling scheme by [Bellare, Hoang and Rogaway 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2382196.2382279), needs a circuit, and a circuit for a RAM program must be able to compute every execution path, so it is at least linear in memory even when the program touches a logarithmic number of cells. Oblivious RAM, introduced for software protection by [Goldreich and Ostrovsky 1996](https://doi.org/10.1145/233551.233553), hides an access pattern at polylogarithmic cost, and [Ostrovsky and Shoup 1997](https://doi.org/10.1145/258533.258606) observed that running an ORAM client inside a secure computation yields secure RAM computation without circuit conversion. That compiler was made concrete for amortized sublinear two-party computation by [Gordon et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2382196.2382251) and for two-server ORAM by [Lu and Ostrovsky 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-36594-2_22), but every scheme in this family pays at least one round per memory access, so a t-step program costs Ω(t) rounds. The single-round ORAM of [Williams and Sion 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2382196.2382229) supplied the trick that would break the dependence: at each level of a hierarchical ORAM the bucket to be probed is one of only two candidates, the real hash location or a dummy, so both can be prepared in advance.

[Lu and Ostrovsky 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-38348-9_42) combined these ingredients into a definition and a candidate construction. The definition mirrors Yao's: algorithms garble the program, garble the input, and evaluate, with a simulator that sees only the running time and the output, and with garbled program size O((|π| + t)·k^O(1)·polylog n). The construction stores every memory bit as one of two PRF-derived labels indexed by the cell's position and the epoch in which it was written, which the hierarchical ORAM's fixed reshuffle schedule makes computable offline; garbles one universal CPU step per time step; and, for each step, garbles a circuit that emulates the ORAM client and emits, as its revealed output, the garbled circuits of a single-round ORAM query. Memory labels flow into those query circuits as input keys, and the query's output flows into the next CPU step under an encoding fixed in advance. The paper's closing section asked for a reusable garbled RAM whose size does not grow with running time.

### The circularity and its repair

The Lu–Ostrovsky proof does not go through. [Gentry et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-55220-5_23) identified the problem: the construction requires a circular use of Yao garbling and PRFs, since the PRF seed that generates memory labels must sit inside garbled circuits that are themselves keyed under labels derived from that seed, and no ordering of hybrids resolves it. Their repair replaces the PRF-derived memory labels with identity-based encryption, so that a reading circuit needs only a public key while the secret material is confined to the writer, and obtains provable security with poly(k)·polylog(n) overhead. The companion approach by Lu and Ostrovsky in the same merged paper keeps one-way functions as the only assumption at the price of poly(k)·n^ε overhead for any constant ε. The two later papers in your own collection summarize this episode the same way: Lu–Ostrovsky needs a circular-security assumption, and the follow-ups removed it with more expensive non-black-box machinery.

### Minimal assumptions and black-box constructions

The assumption gap closed within a year. [Garg et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2746539.2746593) gave the first garbled RAM from one-way functions alone with polylogarithmic overhead in both space and time, still using the primitives non-black-box, and [Garg, Lu and Ostrovsky 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/focs.2015.22) removed even that, giving a black-box construction from one-way functions with polylogarithmic overhead that supports many programs over a persistent database and yields the first constant-round secure RAM computation protocol that is black-box in one-way functions in the OT-hybrid model. Their central device, a network of garbled circuits each able to hand control to one of several successors, is the direct ancestor of the lazy permutation network in EpiGRAM; the thesis in your collection says so explicitly and lists where the newer network improves on it, chiefly by avoiding a factor-κ blowup when passing messages through the network. [Lu and Ostrovsky 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-63715-0_3) extended the black-box construction to parallel RAM programs. The garbled-lookup-table line has recently returned to this question of assumptions from the practical side: [Lin, Lu and Zhou 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-12287-2_3) garble PRFs and lookup tables efficiently from standard PRFs alone, without the circular-correlation-robust hash that Free XOR and its descendants require.

### Adaptivity, succinctness and outsourcing

Two strengthenings of the definition were pursued in parallel. Adaptive security, where inputs and later programs are chosen after the garbled database is sent, is what a persistent outsourced database actually needs. [Garg, Ostrovsky and Srinivasan 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-96878-0_18) achieved it from laconic oblivious transfer ([Cho et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-63715-0_2)), with garbled database and program sizes linear in the database and running time up to polylogarithmic factors, where previously adaptive garbled RAM was known only from obfuscation or in the random-oracle model; as a corollary they obtain the first constant-round maliciously secure computation for persistent RAM programs from standard assumptions. The adaptively secure garbled circuits of [Hemenway et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-53015-3_6) are the circuit-level background. A recent preprint by Barnum, Heath, Kolesnikov and Ostrovsky ([ePrint 2023/1527](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1527), revised 2025, not yet peer reviewed) proves adaptive security for tri-state-circuit garbling in the non-programmable random-oracle model, giving adaptive garbled RAM of size O(T·log³T·log log T·λ) with Boolean-circuit performance matching half gates.

Succinctness answered the open question Lu and Ostrovsky posed. [Gentry et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/focs.2014.50) built private outsourcing of RAM computation in which the client's work is essentially independent of the computation and the server's work is proportional to RAM running time, explicitly addressing that question. Three STOC 2015 papers then obtained succinct garbling or randomized encodings of RAM and Turing-machine computations from indistinguishability obfuscation ([Bitansky et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2746539.2746574); [Canetti et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2746539.2746621); [Koppula, Lewko and Waters 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2746539.2746614)), [Canetti and Holmgren 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2840728.2840765) made the garbled program fully succinct, and [Canetti et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-53644-5_3) and [Ananth et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-53644-5_1) added adaptive security and adaptive soundness for delegated RAM computation. All of these rest on obfuscation, and a succinct garbled RAM from anything weaker remains open. On the input side, the packing of garbled-circuit input keys to O(n + k) bits that Lu and Ostrovsky mention as a communication is [Applebaum et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-40084-1_10).

### Malicious and multiparty security

The first generation of malicious-secure RAM computation bolted cut-and-choose onto garbled RAM. [Hazay and Yanai 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00145-019-09321-3) gave constant-round maliciously secure two-party computation in the RAM model, [Garg et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-53641-4_19) constant-round multiparty RAM computation black-box in one-way functions, and [Miao 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-40186-3_26) a cut-and-choose technique for garbled RAM itself, yielding the first constant-round malicious two-party protocol that runs multiple programs against a persistent database. [Hazay and Lilintal 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-57990-6_12) reframed the primitive as gradual garbled RAM, which needs no separate memory-garbling algorithm and is closer in shape to circuit garbling. These protocols were expensive relative to semi-honest garbled RAM, and the current generation, discussed below, reaches malicious security at semi-honest cost.

## Practical garbled RAM

### Why the foundational constructions were unusable

The asymptotic analysis in the tri-state paper puts the original construction at O(T·log³T·log^c log T·|C_prf|·λ), where |C_prf| is the size of a PRF circuit: every access evaluates PRFs, and in the black-box successors other cryptographic primitives, inside the garbled circuit, and each such evaluation costs a full cipher circuit's worth of garbled tables. EpiGRAM's abstract states the consequence plainly: multiple constructions were known and none was suitable for practice. The practical phase therefore had a single organizing goal, to arrange memory so that labels are moved rather than regenerated from a key, which requires nothing cryptographic inside the circuit. Its toolkit came from a decade of garbled-circuit engineering: half gates ([Zahur, Rosulek and Evans 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-46803-6_8)), oblivious stacks and queues in circuits ([Zahur and Evans 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp.2013.40)), stacked garbling that charges only the longest branch ([Heath and Kolesnikov 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-56880-1_27)), one-hot garbling ([Heath and Kolesnikov 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3460120.3484764)) and garbled circuits with a sublinear evaluator ([Haque et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-06944-4_2)).

### EpiGRAM, NanoGRAM and tri-state circuits

[Heath, Kolesnikov and Ostrovsky 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-06944-4_1) built the first practical garbled RAM around a lazy permutation network that routes labels between garbled circuits in cleartext, incurring amortized O(w·log²n·κ) communication and computation per access for word size w = Ω(log²n) and no factor-κ blowup beyond the labels themselves, together with a custom ORAM designed for garbled circuits rather than an off-the-shelf one. [Park, Lin and Shi 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-30545-0_16) then reached Õ(λ·(W·log N + log³N)) bits per access, which for W = Ω(log²N) is Õ(λ·W·log N), optimal in N up to polyloglog factors and matching the interactive baseline of Circuit ORAM ([Wang, Chan and Shi 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2810103.2813634)) run inside a garbled circuit while removing its logarithmic rounds; they report overtaking EpiGRAM from memory size 2¹³. [Heath, Kolesnikov and Ostrovsky 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-38551-3_5) changed the framing: tri-state circuits, a static circuit model with an undefined wire value and eagerly firing gates whose execution order depends on input, emulate T RAM steps with O(T·log³T·log log T) gates, and garbling a tri-state circuit gives a garbled RAM directly. The same paper's authenticated garbling yields maliciously secure constant-round two-party RAM computation at O(T·log³T·log log T·λ), which it reports as cheaper than all prior work including semi-honest garbled RAM. The tri-state model also formalizes the sequential floor of every garbled RAM: an access cannot begin until its address exists, so steps chain end to end regardless of how much parallel work each contains.

The interactive alternatives remained the yardstick. Path ORAM ([Stefanov et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2508859.2516660)) and Circuit ORAM define the baseline of ORAM inside MPC, Floram ([Doerner and shelat 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3133956.3133967)) showed that linear-work distributed point functions beat sublinear ORAM at practical sizes, and secret-sharing-based oblivious data structures ([Keller and Scholl 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-45608-8_27)) and maliciously secure RAM-MPC ([Keller and Yanai 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-78372-7_4)) are the interactive counterparts. The ORAM lower bound of [Larsen and Nielsen 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-96881-0_18) and its optimal match OptORAMa ([Asharov et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3566049)) fix the target the garbled constructions are now approaching: O(W·log N) bandwidth per access.

## The current frontier

### Shaving the last factors

Three 2025 and 2026 constructions attack the gap between Õ(λ·W·log N) and optimal ORAM bandwidth, and they split along the public-key versus symmetric-key line. [Gu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-01913-4_8) use decisional Diffie–Hellman to obtain a garbled RAM that asymptotically matches the best interactive RAM-model two-party computation with no polyloglog gap, has optimal dependence on database size up to arbitrarily small super-constant factors, and delivers an order-of-magnitude online-time improvement over interactive RAM-model 2PC on an 8 GB dataset. [Liu et al. 2026](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/761), accepted to CCS 2026, recover the same O(n·lg³n·λ)·ω(1) bound under symmetric-key assumptions through a new garbled merge of size (w + 1)·n·λ bits, improving the tri-state result by a lg lg n factor and beating the DDH-based merge by about 3× in communication. The security-parameter factor itself falls only with public-key tools so far. Zebra ([Gu, Ghoshal and Shi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-25324-8_10)) garbles arithmetic RAM under decisional composite residuosity at O(T·W·log N) bandwidth for suitably large words, the RAM analogue of constant-rate arithmetic garbling, with a Boolean extension at O(T·W·(log N + λ)) and a measured 10.1× bandwidth and 3.5× end-to-end gain over the previous best on a 256 MB database with 4 kB entries; the arithmetic garbling it builds on is [Heath 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-58740-5_1). [Fickes, He and Lin 2026](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1998), accepted to TCC 2026, reach O(T·(W + λ)·log N) + poly(λ) communication for Boolean RAM from homomorphic secret sharing under circular-power variants of DDH or ring LWE, which for W ≥ λ matches optimal ORAM bandwidth and removes a multiplicative λ from the random-oracle-model construction of Liu et al.

### Lookup tables, garbled PIR and multiparty RAM

A parallel line treats random access to a public table as a gate. [Heath, Kolesnikov and Ng 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-58740-5_7) garble an N-row lookup table with a logarithmic number of ciphertexts, [Ng and Kolesnikov 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3719027.3744831) obtain garbled private information retrieval from table-only stacking with far lower bandwidth than the lookup-table construction it supersedes, and the minimal-assumption garbled PRFs and tables of Lin, Lu and Zhou belong here as well. On the multiparty side, [Heath et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-01884-7_18) give constant-round dishonest-majority MPC whose communication scales linearly in the number of parties from oblivious transfer and a random oracle, and by garbling tri-state circuits extend it to RAM programs at O(n·T·log³T·log log T·κ) total bits with malicious security. Interactive random access has advanced in step: [Peceny et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-91829-2_3) systematize permutation correlations for batched oblivious access in two-party computation, and [Braun et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3576915.3623115) give fast three-party computation for RAM programs.

### Beyond garbling

Two non-garbling routes to non-interactive RAM computation now exist alongside garbled RAM. [Lin, Mook and Wichs 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3564246.3585175) obtain fully homomorphic RAM computation from ring LWE via doubly efficient private information retrieval, and [Dong et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-58723-8_7) give laconic function evaluation, functional encryption and obfuscation for RAMs with sublinear computation. Both inherit heavier assumptions and, at present, far heavier constants than garbling, but they are the constructions to watch if the goal is client work independent of running time, which garbled RAM by definition cannot offer.

## Where the field stands

What is settled: garbled RAM exists from one-way functions, black-box, with polylogarithmic overhead; practical constructions run at a few logarithms times λ per access; malicious security and dishonest-majority multiparty security cost no more than semi-honest garbled RAM once tri-state circuits are used; and adaptive security is available from laconic OT or, pending review, from a non-programmable random oracle.

What is in motion: the per-access bandwidth is converging on optimal ORAM bandwidth, but the two ways of getting there disagree on assumptions. Symmetric-key constructions reach O(n·lg³n·λ)·ω(1); removing λ altogether currently needs DCR or homomorphic secret sharing. Whether a λ-free garbled RAM exists from one-way functions or a random oracle is the sharpest open question, and it mirrors the older question of constant-rate garbling for Boolean circuits.

What is open beyond that: matching the log N lower bound exactly, with no ω(1) or polyloglog slack, under minimal assumptions; adaptive security at practical cost from standard assumptions rather than a random oracle; succinct garbled RAM from anything short of obfuscation, the question Lu and Ostrovsky asked in 2013 and which no later work has answered without iO; practical parallel garbled RAM, where the only construction is theoretical; and compilers, since the implementations that exist, EpiGRAM's and the CCS 2023 variable-instruction-set work in your collection, garble hand-written programs rather than compiled ones.
